§ 1 · Introduction

When High Accuracy
Isn't Enough

Drug-drug interactions pose serious clinical risks. GNNs have emerged as powerful predictors—but a high AUROC only tells us a model can discriminate interacting from non-interacting pairs. It tells us nothing about whether those predictions are stable under small input perturbations.

Primary Hypothesis: Drug interaction predictions produced by graph neural networks are highly sensitive to small perturbations in learned node embeddings. Models that achieve strong predictive accuracy may mask representational instability that would concern clinicians.
🔬

The Research Question

Are GNN-based DDI predictions robust to small perturbations in learned representations? We apply Gaussian noise and dimensional dropout to frozen embeddings post-hoc—isolating embedding space sensitivity from message-passing dynamics.

⚠️

Why It Matters Clinically

If tiny changes in an embedding vector cause large shifts in predicted probabilities or completely reshuffle ranked interaction lists, clinicians should be cautious trusting those predictions in decision support systems—regardless of reported test-set accuracy.

§ 5 · Stability Analysis — Gaussian Noise

The Stability Framework
& Results

We freeze trained weights, extract drug embeddings, and apply perturbations post-hoc. Each level is repeated 10 times with different seeds. We measure three components combined into a Stability Score.

Gaussian Noise: z' = z + ε, ε ~ N(0, σ²I), σ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30}

Stability Score(σ) = ⅓ × [(1 − mean|Δp|) + mean Spearman ρ + mean Jaccard top-20]  Range: [0, 1]

§ 8 · Key Findings & Conclusions

Six Findings That Change
How We Evaluate GNNs

Concrete conclusions from our stability audit — with direct implications for clinical deployment of GNN-based drug interaction prediction systems.

1
Accuracy ≠ Stability
RGCN achieves 0.917 AUROC vs GCN's 0.827 yet shows similar or slightly worse stability (SS 0.390 vs 0.470 at σ=0.30). Heterogeneous context that improves accuracy does not automatically improve robustness.
2
The Accuracy–Stability Trade-Off Is Real
Ablation shows Disease-Drug edges improve AUROC by 1.6 points but decrease Stability Score by 4.3 points. The most informative auxiliary structure makes embedding spaces more sensitive to perturbation — a classic robustness-performance tension.
3
The Cold-Start Stability Paradox
When accuracy collapses in cold-start settings (GCN: 0.827→0.098, RGCN: 0.917→0.314), stability scores hold or improve. Less specialized embeddings are paradoxically more robust — specialization and stability are inversely related.
4
Dropout vs. Gaussian: Different Fragility Modes
Both models maintain substantially higher stability scores under dimensional dropout than under Gaussian noise at matched severities (e.g., 0.680 vs 0.470 at the most extreme level). Structured perturbations reveal different fragility than additive noise.
5
Heuristics Can Outperform Learned GNNs
Common-neighbor (AUROC 0.887) beats the structure-only GCN (0.827). For dense graphs like DDI networks, simple topological signals are strong baselines that GNNs must overcome with auxiliary heterogeneous data — not just more architecture.
6
Stability Auditing Must Complement Accuracy
Deploying GNN-based DDI prediction in clinical systems requires Stability Score certificates across perturbation levels — not just test-set AUROC. Both metrics are necessary for confident deployment in safety-critical applications.
Conclusion: We built a heterogeneous DDI knowledge graph from four SNAP datasets and trained both a structure-only GCN (AUROC 0.827) and a multi-relational RGCN (AUROC 0.917). Our stability analysis reveals both models exhibit comparable sensitivity to embedding perturbation — the RGCN's accuracy advantage does not confer representational robustness. Edge-type ablation further shows that the most accuracy-boosting graph structure slightly decreases stability. These findings argue for stability auditing as an essential complement to standard accuracy evaluation in safety-critical GNN applications.
